The plan on one page

The bet. In Greater Grand Forks nobody local ranks a real leaf-cleanup page, nobody publishes a leaf or gutter price, no junk hauler offers a live estimate or online booking, and East Grand Forks has no real local result for junk or leaves at all. The site already automates estimate → booking → $50 deposit → confirmation → owner board → crew portal → mileage → receipts. So the next 60 days go to the two things that move a Google local pack — a completely built Google Business Profile (≈25–32% of pack weight) and steady review velocity (≈20%) — plus five unique service pages, while the truck works Saturdays and two evenings a week around the W-2 job.

The position. Three services, one truck, flat prices shown before the call, booked in a minute with a $50 deposit that comes off the bill. Leaves priced by the lot; junk by the truck bed; single-story gutters from the ground with no ladder on the siding. The Fargo franchises drive 75 miles; we are on Belmont Road.

The money, in one line
A job keeps about (solo, blended fall mix). The fixed nut the business must clear is a month, so jobs a month covers it and everything past that is profit before tax. Four jobs a Saturday for nine Saturdays (36 jobs) clears about $3,900 after tax; six a week (54 jobs) about $6,600. The full ladder is in §8.
Five things block hauling legally, and they happen this week (§2, §9)
  1. Bind the general-liability policy (NEXT quote ≈$85/mo) — the hauler permit, landlord COIs and Local Services Ads all need it.
  2. File the Grand Forks private-hauler permit (City Code §13-0406.1, $75, council approval, bond + proof of liability). "Rubbish" in that code includes lawn clippings and discarded furniture — leaf hauls and junk hauls both need it. Until it issues: sell rake-to-the-curb leaf jobs (no hauling on city streets) and haul only outside city limits.
  3. Get a written yes/no from GF Finance (701-746-2664) on whether hanging a door hanger without knocking is "door-to-door sales" under Ordinance 4707 — before the hangers go out.
  4. Call Progressive and disclose business use — a personal auto policy typically excludes hauling for pay; a claim during a paid haul is deniable today.
  5. EIN the day the LLC shows active (~Sept 18), then the Minnesota Tax ID before any East Grand Forks job — Minnesota taxes all three services (8.375% sales tax on leaf and gutter work, 9.75% solid-waste tax on junk hauls); North Dakota taxes none of them.

What breaks first — in order

  1. The permit calendar, not demand. City Council meets the 1st and 3rd Monday at 5:30 pm — Sept 21, Oct 5, Oct 19. Until §13-0406.1 issues, every in-city haul (leaf and junk) is off; in-city work is gutters and, from Oct 19, rake-to-curb. Leaves aren't down before the ~Oct 6 first 28°F anyway. Get the agenda-submission deadline from the City Clerk Monday and make the Oct 5 agenda; miss it and the permit lands Oct 20.
  2. Your weekday hours. 18 jobs is 58 owner-hours a week solo, 40 with a helper on every job — plus the Monday desk hour and 12–15 hours a week of hanging that someone has to do. Weekends plus two evenings is ~21 hours → 6–8 jobs a week. Nothing bridges Sept 21–Oct 11 except leave you haven't requested. Rule: decide on Oct 3 — if 25+ deposit-paid bookings sit on the board, take leave from Oct 5; if not, run the weekend plan. Check the W-2 handbook's outside-employment clause first.
  3. The literature-drop ruling. ~70% of modeled leads are hangers. No written ruling under Ordinance 4707 = 0 in-city hangers; each week of slip removes 15–22 leads.
  4. The helper's week is 52–56 hours, not 40, if the same person hangs. Overtime at 1.5× or a second part-time hanger: loaded wages are ≈$1,100 a week, not $806.
  5. Booking-to-completion lag. September leads are October dates; the first arithmetically possible 18-week is Oct 12–17, and the 25th-percentile first snow is that same week.
  6. Saturday's dump window. Landfill 8–11; the yard-waste site appears closed Saturday (unconfirmed — 701-738-8740). Saturday = two hauls tipped by 11 plus one no-haul job (gutter or rake-to-curb).
  7. Lead volume itself. Google is capped by search volume — roughly 120 junk-removal searches a month in this metro — at 5–9 leads a week no matter the budget; hangers and the block-deal loop are the volume.
  8. Weather. Median first measurable snow Oct 26; median 1-inch Nov 8. After Nov 1 the sun sets at 5:09 and the landfill still closes at 4:00, so a solo unchained 3-job day no longer fits.
  9. Cash before Oct 3. ≈$3.5–4k goes out (print $903, ads ~$440, Nextdoor $140, GL, permit + bond, WSI $250 minimum, first payroll ~$813 on Oct 2) against maybe $2–3k collected. Not fatal with W-2 income; it means the 5,000-hanger run waits for the calibration read.

The mechanism, per dollar of job profit

Trading — the mechanics that matter, no advice on what to trade

Trader tax status needs all three of: profit sought from daily price movements, substantial activity, and continuity and regularity — facts and circumstances, no bright-line trade count. The Tax Court has denied it at 200–535 trades a year when trades fell on fewer than half of the trading days, with multi-week gaps, or with holding periods of weeks (Holsinger 2008, Endicott 2013, Nelson 2013 — and in Nelson the taxpayer also had a W-2 job). A full-time job is a strong negative on "time devoted". Treat yourself as an investor unless the CPA says otherwise after a full year of day-by-day records — hours and fills, starting now, are the only evidence that would ever change that. Investor consequences: short-term gains at ordinary rates (12% or 22% here, plus ND above the 0% band); long-term at 0% up to $49,450 of taxable income then 15%; net losses capped at $3,000 a year against other income, carried forward indefinitely; the wash-sale rule disallows a loss when substantially identical stock is bought within 30 days either side — including in an IRA, and across accounts, which the broker's 1099-B does not track for you; platform and data costs are not deductible for an investor. §475(f) mark-to-market (ordinary gains and losses on Form 4797, no wash sales, no $3,000 cap) exists only for a trader; for 2026 the election window closed on April 15, 2026 and late elections are essentially never allowed; for 2027 the statement attaches to the 2026 return or extension by April 15, 2027, with Form 3115 on the 2027 return. A "trading LLC" does not create trader status — it only changes election timing and adds a return — so there is none. Reserve: sweep the marginal rate (12%, 22% above the line) of each month's realized net gain into the tax account's "trading" line; there is no withholding on gains. Never use trading capital as working capital: withdrawing means selling (gains taxed now, losses capped), margin makes a withdrawal a forced liquidation, business bills are dated and markets aren't, and a margin account under FINRA's $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum loses the ability to day trade (a 2025 amendment to that rule was pending — status unknown). The brokerage account never receives a business deposit and never pays a business bill.

Rental, S-corp, holding company — the standing decisions, with their triggers

  • Rental: Schedule E, no SE tax; a loss offsets the W-2 up to $25,000 a year if you actively participate (approve tenants, set rent) — fully available at this income. QBI on rent needs the 250-hour safe harbor and its own books, or a real §162 business. Conventional loans close in a person's name; Fannie Mae allows a later deed into an LLC you control without triggering the due-on-sale clause, but the LLC has to deed it back to refinance; any other lender needs written consent. ND LLC $135 + $50 a year; no ND transfer tax; county recording fee and the umbrella premium are unknown — quote them. NIIT (3.8%) starts at $200k of income — not binding.
  • S-corp: SE tax on $7,500 of net is ≈$1,060; payroll and an 1120-S cost more than that. Revisit when combined Schedule C net is over ~$50–60k two years running and projected SE savings exceed ~$3k a year after a reasonable salary. Note an S-corp adds WSI and unemployment on your own wages in ND, which a Schedule C owner never pays.
  • Holding company: no. ND taxes residents on all income wherever earned; a Wyoming LLC doing business in ND registers as a foreign LLC and pays ND fees anyway — one more return, one more registered agent, no tax saved. Revisit when two operating entities each hold meaningful equity (rule of thumb > $50k each) or a second rental LLC exists. Outside money into LuxeForge would mean a Delaware C-corp for the SaaS alone, not a holdco.
  • BOI / FinCEN: nothing to file — domestic LLCs were exempted in March 2025.
